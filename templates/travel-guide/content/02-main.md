## Morning · Baixa to Alfama

Walk north-to-south through the grid before turning east into the older city. The shift in street pattern is immediate: broad axes become stairs, alleys and small terraces.

<div class="travel-callout">
<span class="travel-callout-label">PHOTO NOTE</span>
Look backward when climbing. Many of Lisbon's strongest compositions appear behind you rather than ahead.
</div>

## Lunch · keep it simple

Choose one place rather than building the day around reservations. The guide layout keeps recommendations compact so they do not overpower the route.

<div class="eat-card">
<strong>What to look for</strong>
<ul>
<li>A short menu</li>
<li>Tables occupied by locals at lunch</li>
<li>Grilled fish or a daily plate</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Afternoon · the river resets the pace

The flat riverfront gives your legs a break after the hills. Use it as a transition rather than a destination in itself.

> The best city days alternate compression and release: narrow streets, open water, then height again.

## Evening · climb to Graça

Arrive before sunset rather than at sunset. The final hour is better when there is time to watch the light move across the city.

<div class="practical-box">
<strong>Practical</strong>
<p><b>Shoes:</b> grip matters on polished stone.</p>
<p><b>Water:</b> carry it before the climb.</p>
<p><b>Transit backup:</b> use tram or rideshare only when the route stops being enjoyable.</p>
</div>
